Starbucks: an empathy map for the inconsistent morning

A short research exercise on service consistency — what happens to a loyal customer when the same order is only right seven times out of ten.

The study

Jane Lee is 26, a travel nurse in Chicago, and a loyal Starbucks customer — she starts every morning there regardless of which city work has put her in. That loyalty is exactly what makes her a useful subject: she has enough data points to notice when the experience isn't consistent.

Gains

  • Her hazelnut latte is made the same way roughly seven times out of ten.
  • The rewards programme is genuinely worth having.
  • Coffee arrives within a reasonable time.

Pains

  • Food and drink handling — lids touched with unwashed hands after contact with money, coworkers, and other guests. As a travel nurse, getting sick on the job is a professional risk, not an inconvenience.
  • Uncertainty about whether rewards benefits work the same at every location.
  • She avoids the food entirely: dry, or a fly in the display case.

The empathy map splits what she sees, hears, says and does, and thinks and feels — and the gap between the last two is where the problem lives. She keeps coming back, and she has quietly stopped buying half the menu.
