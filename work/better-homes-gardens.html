Better Homes & Gardens: editorial that holds at every width

A content-heavy publisher lives or dies on whether the reader keeps reading. This was a responsive rework built around hierarchy and typography first, then wireframes and comps that survive the jump from desktop to phone.

The brief

Better Homes & Gardens publishes long, image-rich, practical articles — the kind people read halfway through on a laptop and finish on a phone in the kitchen. The design problem is consistency: the same story has to stay navigable, readable, and scannable across both, without the mobile version quietly becoming a worse experience.

Approach

I ran multi-platform user research and turned the findings into interface decisions that support accessibility and inclusive design, then mapped the full user journey and system flows to find where readers hit friction and where the information architecture and navigation could carry more weight.

From there: responsive wireframes and prototypes in Figma, holding usability consistent across web and mobile, with the type system doing most of the work. Long-form editorial needs a scale that keeps body copy comfortable, section headings findable, and captions and pull quotes distinct from the article text at every breakpoint.

I facilitated stakeholder reviews to align on the design specifications and support developer handoff — the point where a responsive design usually either survives or quietly loses its rules.
