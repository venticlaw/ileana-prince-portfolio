How I work

How I want every project to run. Local Roots is the one that got all the way through step four.

  1. Ask

    Interviews and surveys with real users — a farmer at a market, five testers on the phone — before a single screen exists.

  2. Map

    Personas, journeys, sitemaps, and feature lists that turn scattered findings into something a team can build against.

  3. Build

    Sketches to wireframes to high-fidelity Figma prototypes, with a style guide so the details stay consistent.

  4. Test & revise

    Task-based usability sessions, then visible changes — as on Local Roots. Where a project stopped short of testing, its page says so and names what I'd measure.

About

I design interfaces for people who are busy, distracted, or new to whatever they're trying to do — which is most of us, most of the time.

I hold a B.S. in User Experience Design from Full Sail University, where I spent three months building Local Roots end to end: researching sustainable agriculture, interviewing farmers in Texas and at a local market, and iterating a prototype through two full rounds of user testing. My design philosophy centers on empathy and innovation, and I draw a lot of it from nature and from ordinary, everyday interactions.

Accessibility isn't a pass at the end of a project for me. It shows up in the type sizes, the hit targets, the contrast ratios, and whether a visual indicator carries meaning on its own without relying on color — the things I gathered into the iOS Mobile Toolkit.

Day to day I work as an Enrollment Services Advisor at Full Sail University, managing a multi-stage pipeline in Salesforce and translating requirements between admissions, financial aid, and academic teams. It is not a design title, but it is the same work: making a complicated process legible to the person moving through it.
